Cortical surface annotation in the browser

Delineate ROIs on a cortical surface, and export them.

Load a FreeSurfer or GIfTI surface — folded, inflated, spherical, or an unfolded flat — and mark regions by clicking along their border. The points are joined by the shortest path across the mesh, so the border follows the surface rather than a straight line through it. ROIs are drawn in sequence and partition the surface: each one's border becomes the edge the next is closed against, so neighbouring ROIs share a boundary exactly instead of having overlapping vertices.

Your surfaces, overlays and labels never leave this machine — there is no server to send them to.

  • FreeSurfer surfaces: lh.pial, lh.white, lh.inflated, flat surfaces
  • GIfTI .surf.gii, .mz3, and more

How it works

1

Load a surface

Pick a file or drop it on the viewer. Load several and switch between them; an ROI drawn on the inflated surface is still there on the folded one.

2

Click the border

Nothing is traced while you click, hold and hover around the surface, so you can rotate freely. Close the border and fill it. On a cut surface the edge of the flat surface closes it for you — two points can be enough.

3

Save and export

Saved ROIs become the edges the next ones are drawn against, and can be reopened to adjust. Export each as a .label or .label.gii.

Everything runs in this browser tab — no upload, no account, no server. MIT licensed · rendering by NiiVue

      How shared overlays work

      Sharing applies to overlays loaded before and after the checkbox is ticked. Matching surfaces share the colour map, range and visibility; removing an overlay removes it from all of them. Unticking keeps existing groups shared and leaves new overlays on the surface they are loaded onto.

      Optional. Every overlay is drawn only where the mask is non-zero. A file with "curv" or "curvature" in its name (lh.curv, hemi-L_curv.shape.gii) is treated as anatomy and always shown; for any other file, tick "Always show this overlay". A file with "mask" in its name can just be dropped on the viewer.

      03

      Annotate

      Trace a closed region or mark individual vertices.

      Click to place border points around the region. Nothing is traced until you close the ROI, so you can rotate freely while placing them.

      Points are joined in the order you place them, not by which are nearest each other. Work consistently along the border in one direction. Close ROI joins the last point back to the first.

      Draw only the part of the border crossing the flat surface: both ends are extended to the nearest edge, which closes the region. Two points are enough.

      Markers are drawn on top of the view at a fixed size, each with a contrasting outline so it stays visible over any surface or overlay. Landmarks use the same colour and the other shape.

      0.55

      The interior is detected automatically; if it is ambiguous you are asked to click inside the region you want. Closing on the edge takes the smaller side.

      With Include boundary vertices off, a saved ROI does not own the row it was drawn along, so the next ROI drawn against it claims that row. Tick it before saving to keep the border where you clicked it.

      Click to place a landmark. Click an existing one to remove it.

        04

        Saved ROIs

        Name, organise and revisit completed regions.

          Restores ROIs saved by this app — a .surfannotate.json session, or a .label.gii it exported — with their border points, so they can be edited or added to. They are appended below any ROIs already listed.

          How ROI partitioning works

          Saved ROIs never overlap. The list is a priority order: where two ROIs would share vertices, the one higher in the list keeps them and the lower one is trimmed to what is left. Because an ROI is stored as its border, moving a border re-shapes every ROI below it automatically. A saved ROI's rim also acts as an edge for the next one, so you only need to click the new border, not the shared one. Use ▲▼ to change the order, ✎ to reopen a border, or select a name to export that ROI.
